## S1. Role mapping to decision dialogue acts (DDA) and IBIS (our interpretation)
Source: `docs/schema_mapping.md`. Previously Table I in the paper; the paper now gives a one-sentence summary.

| Role | Closest DDA (Fernandez et al. 2008) | Closest IBIS element | Match |
|---|---|---|---|
| proposal | resolution proposal (RP) | position | partial: RP is tied to the adopted option; ours is not |
| rationale | none | argument | IBIS only |
| evidence | none | argument (the "evidence" half) | partial |
| challenge | none | opposing argument | IBIS only |
| reconsideration | issue (I), re-raised | issue re-raised | partial |
| resolution | resolution (RP / RR), agreement (A) | chosen position | yes |

## S7. Worked example: what cited-lineage F1 does and does not measure
Referenced in Section IV ("Illustration of metric scope"). Case: the Fig. 1 question (TEST `set_01037-q06`, why_changed): "Why was the original decision about which carrier handles outbound parcels in the south-east changed?" Gold lineage: B2, B4, B5, B6, B7, B8. Built by `paper/make_supp_example.py` from the gold file and our scored answer; both versions are scored with the frozen `eval/lineage_f1.score_question`.

**Reference answer (gold).** Before: all south-east parcels with Parcelwise, a national carrier, on a 12-month contract at 4.10 pounds a parcel, because Dmitri argues that the current average is 4.85 pounds, the region ships about 18,000 parcels a quarter, and the move saves roughly 13,500 pounds a quarter. Triggered by: Arjun (evidence): Arjun reports that in the first five weeks Parcelwise delivered 81 percent on time against 96 percent with the regional couriers, leaving 640 late parcels. Diego (challenge): Diego objects that late deliveries cost more than the saving, with 1,900 pounds of customer credits in five weeks and complaints from three enterprise customers. Aisha (reconsideration): Aisha reopens the carrier decision after the late-delivery figures. After: a split between two regional couriers, Eastline and Southgate, by postcode zone at about 4.35 pounds a parcel, because Diego argues for the regional couriers because Eastline and Southgate average 4.35 pounds, guarantee 95 percent on time with credits for misses, saving about 9,000 pounds a quarter, and have no minimum-volume clause where Parcelwise requires 15,000 parcels.

### (a) Our pipeline's actual answer (TEST output, unedited)

**Answer.** The original decision was changed because the figures showed worse performance by Parcelwise, leading to a reconsideration and a shift to Eastline and Southgate.

1. In meeting 2, Arjun raised concerns about Parcelwise's performance, noting that it was landing only eighty-one percent on time, compared to ninety-six percent for regional couriers, resulting in about six hundred and forty late parcels. [M2:27]
2. Aisha acknowledged the objection but decided not to change the decision based on incomplete figures, stating that Parcelwise would remain as the carrier for now and the issue would be revisited next week. [M2:93]
3. In meeting 3, Aisha reconsidered the decision after realizing that the previous figures were incomplete and worse than initially thought. [M3:32]
4. Arjun clarified that the previous thirteen percent figure was misleading because it did not include credits, and the actual performance was worse. [M3:43-45]
5. Aisha then decided to change the original decision, moving the south-east parcels to Eastline and Southgate, split by postcode zone, and giving Parcelwise their notice. [M3:74]
6. The original decision was based on incomplete and misleading figures, which were later corrected and found to be worse than initially thought. [M3:32] [M3:43-45]
7. The change in decision was made after a full reconsideration of the figures and the impact of the performance data on the original decision. [M3:32] [M3:74]
8. The decision was ultimately changed due to the corrected figures and the realization that Parcelwise's performance was worse than initially believed. [M3:32] [M3:43-45] [M3:74]

Score: 5 items cited; precision 0.60, recall 0.50, cited-lineage F1 0.55.

### (b) CONSTRUCTED FOR ILLUSTRATION: wrong reason, same citations
Not a system output. Each sentence keeps the citations of the matching sentence in (a), but the stated reason for the change is cost, not late deliveries, which contradicts the gold lineage.

**Answer.** The original decision was changed because Parcelwise turned out to cost more than the regional couriers.

1. In meeting 2, Arjun reported that Parcelwise's price per parcel was higher than the team had expected. [M2:27]
2. Aisha kept Parcelwise for now because the cost figures were not yet complete. [M2:93]
3. In meeting 3, Aisha reopened the decision because of the higher cost. [M3:32]
4. Arjun stated that the earlier saving had been overstated and that Parcelwise was the more expensive option. [M3:43-45]
5. Aisha moved the south-east parcels to Eastline and Southgate to reduce shipping costs. [M3:74]
6. The original decision rested on a price estimate that turned out to be too low. [M3:32] [M3:43-45]
7. The change followed a review of the carriers' prices. [M3:32] [M3:74]
8. The decision was changed to cut costs. [M3:32] [M3:43-45] [M3:74]

Score: 5 items cited; precision 0.60, recall 0.50, cited-lineage F1 0.55, identical to (a).

### What this shows
Cited-lineage F1 depends only on which lines are cited, so (a) and (b) score the same although (b) explains the change wrongly. The metric measures evidence localization; it does not check what the explanation says about the cited lines. (b) was not run through the correctness judge or the NLI checker; whether they would catch it is not claimed here.

## S9. Method details of our pipeline (frozen configuration)
Referenced in the paper's Section V. Source of truth: `argdecisionlineage/` (graph, linking, retrieval, answer prompt), `synthgen/extract_recap.py` (extraction prompt), `eval/phase3_topk.py` (cap); parameters as frozen in `reports/FREEZE.md` section 13 (`GRAPH_PARAMS`, `LINK_PARAMS` in `argdecisionlineage/retrieve.py`).

**1. Extraction (one LLM call per meeting, meetings in order).** The prompt shows the meeting as `[index] speaker: text`, a role guide with one definition per role and contrastive examples for the confusable pairs (challenge vs rationale, reconsideration vs proposal or challenge, rationale vs evidence; the examples are invented, not from the benchmark), and a **recap**. The recap lists the decisions the pipeline itself extracted from meetings 1..k-1 of the same series, grouped by its own decision names (at most the 12 most recent decisions, at most 6 events each, preferring proposals, resolutions, reconsiderations and challenges); each line gives the meeting number, role, speaker, option and the first 160 characters of the cited utterance, under the header "from your own earlier analysis; it may contain mistakes". Gold is never read. Each returned link has `role`, `utterance_idxs` (1-3 consecutive utterances by one speaker, key utterance first), `speaker`, `confidence`, `decision` (a 4-8 word name) and `option`. Long meetings are chunked by the pre-registered rule (FREEZE section 9).

**2. Confidence and cap.** `confidence` is the LLM's own score in [0, 1] (0.5 if missing). Per meeting, the 4 links with the highest confidence are kept (ties: earlier utterance); K = 4 was chosen on DEV from {4, 6, 8, 12}.

**3. Decision linking.** Each link is embedded (all-MiniLM-L6-v2, normalized) over its window: the cited utterances plus one neighboring utterance on each side. Links are processed in time order. A link joins the existing decision whose centroid (sum of member embeddings) has cosine >= 0.4 with it, provided the Jaccard overlap between the speakers of its meeting and the speakers of the decision's meetings is >= 0.2. Otherwise a non-proposal joins the decision most recently active in the same meeting within 60 utterances; otherwise it opens a new decision. The `decision` name from extraction is not used by the linker.

**4. Version rules (rules "v4").** Options are compared by content-token containment |A ∩ B| / min(|A|, |B|) (stop words removed): >= 0.4 same option, 0.2-0.4 modified, < 0.2 different.
- R1 supersede: a resolution adopting a different option closes the current version and opens a new one.
- R2 reaffirm: a resolution matching the current version adds no version; "keep / stick with / stay with / still / remain" with no newer proposal is a reaffirmation.
- R3 modify: a modified option, or "keep X" followed by a qualifier clause (", with ...", "except ...") adding >= 3 new content tokens, opens a version of kind *modified*.
- R4 restore: matching an earlier, non-current version (or an explicit revert: "go back to", "switch back", "revert", "undo") opens a version of kind *restored*.
- R5: a decision with no resolution has no version.
- R6 tentative: a resolution with confidence < 0.9, or phrased as a hypothetical ("maybe we should", "what if", "should we", "could we", "how about", "do we want to", or ending in "?"), never creates or supersedes a version; a request to a person ("Sam, can you send it?") is not treated as a question.
- Option inference: a resolution's own words are the adopted option only if they overlap (containment >= 0.2) with the pending option or the current version; otherwise an implicit resolution ("go ahead") adopts the latest proposal since the last resolution.
- One decision event per decision per meeting: a later decisive resolution in the same meeting replaces that meeting's new version instead of chaining another. Validity is "as of the end of meeting k".

**5. Routing (rule-based, from the question templates).** Intents: `as_of`, `final`, `decisions_about`, `change_over_time`, `why_changed` (three templates), `who_proposed`, `evidence_to_reconsider`, `meetings_contributed`, `full_lineage`, `who_proposed_option`; anything else is `other` (handled like `final`). The topic phrase and, where present, the meeting number are taken from the template.

**6. Anchoring.** Candidates are decisions with at least one proposal or resolution. Each is described by its link texts (proposals and resolutions first). Score = 0.5 x cosine(topic, description) + 0.5 x idf-weighted share of topic tokens found in the description (idf over the candidates' descriptions). The best candidate is the anchor.

**7. Link selection per intent** (positions are utterance positions in the series; "between" means after one resolution and before the next):
- `final` / `other`: the resolution that created the current version, the rationales and evidence since the previous version's resolution, and any later resolutions; with no version, the proposals and rationales (and a "no decision yet" note).
- `as_of` k: the version valid at the end of meeting k, its resolution and the rationales and evidence before it; with none, the proposals, rationales and challenges up to meeting k.
- `why_changed`: for each pair of consecutive versions (or the pair whose new version starts at meeting k, if named), the rationales before the old resolution, the challenges, evidence, reconsiderations and rationales between the old and new resolutions, and the new resolution. With fewer than two versions: challenges, evidence, reconsiderations, non-initial resolutions and rationales, plus a note that the graph records no change.
- `evidence_to_reconsider`: evidence, challenges and reconsiderations between the last two resolutions (or after the only one); if none fall there, all of them.
- `full_lineage` / `meetings_contributed`: all links of the decision.
- `change_over_time` / `decisions_about`: all resolutions.
- `who_proposed`: the first proposal; `who_proposed_option`: the proposal (else rationale) closest to the named option (0.5 cosine + 0.5 token overlap).
- Cap: at most 12 links, keeping by role priority (resolution, reconsideration, evidence, challenge, rationale, proposal) then time, and returned in time order.
- Vector fallback: for `final`, `as_of`, `who_proposed` and `who_proposed_option`, if the anchor score is < 0.35 or the decision has no version, the 4 utterances whose one-neighbor windows are most similar to the question are added as untyped excerpts.

**8. Answer and citation format.** The generator (qwen3:14b, temperature 0, seed 7, 700 output tokens) sees the question; a header listing the decision versions (number, kind, from and until which meeting, option) or a note that none exists, plus notes for "no change", "as of meeting k" and "no decision yet"; and the evidence as numbered lines `[L1] meeting 2 (2025-04-17), Arjun (evidence): text`, in time order. It returns JSON `{"answer": one sentence, "sentences": [{"text", "cites": ["L1", ...]}]}` with 1-8 chronological sentences, each citing evidence ids. The harness maps each id back to its meeting and utterance indexes; those are the cited items scored by cited-lineage F1. The NLI checker marks unsupported sentences; under the frozen *flag* policy nothing is removed.
