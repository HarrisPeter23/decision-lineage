# Writer prompt v3: one meeting of an internal corporate meeting transcript

You are writing ONE meeting for a research benchmark of synthetic corporate meetings. All people and companies are fictional.
Your spec file gives the team, cast, date, topic, this meeting's events, the noise level, and any earlier transcripts.
Read only that spec file and this prompt. You know nothing about what happens after this meeting, and nobody in the room does either.
Do not hint at, set up, or lean toward any later outcome. Nobody foreshadows, and no objection is made suspiciously neat.

## Setting
An internal business meeting of colleagues in one team of a company. Decisions have business stakes: budgets and approvals, vendors and
contracts, customers and revenue, headcount, deadlines, compliance, tooling. Use natural corporate texture: status updates, action items,
"let's take that offline", sign-off from finance or legal, calendar chatter. Conversational, never a slide-deck voice.

## Output
Save the meeting to the output path you were given, then reply only "saved". The file is plain text with one utterance per line:

Name: spoken words
Name: spoken words that realize event B1 @B1

- No header, no blank lines, no line breaks inside an utterance, no markdown, no stage directions, no brackets, no narration.
- Tags go at the very end of a line after a space: ` @B4`, or ` @B4,B5` for two events on one line.

## How to tag
- Tag only the 1-3 consecutive utterances by the named speaker that realize the event, most important first. Reactions get no tags.
- Every listed tag appears, in the listed order.
- A decision happens at the tagged utterance, never earlier. Between events the current decision stays in force. Do not state or imply any
  decision that is not listed.

## Dialogue
1. Real spoken language, not a summary: fillers (um, uh, you know, I mean), false starts, self-corrections, trailing off with "-", people
   cutting each other off (an utterance ends with "-" and the next speaker jumps in), short turns, backchannels ("right", "mm").
2. Follow the noise settings: interruptions, share of unrelated talk, false starts, pronoun-heavy reference ("that", "it", "the thing from
   last time" instead of repeating the option's name), tangent, and target length.
3. LENGTH: between 60 and 120 utterances, never more than 120, and within about 10 of the target. Most utterances are 3-25 words; a few are longer. Count before saving.
4. IMPLICIT resolutions: the tagged speaker must NOT name the option. Use a bare agreement or an action that presupposes the decision, in a
   fresh form of your own. What is being agreed to must be clear from the preceding turns. Never use "let's go with that" or "let's do that".
   All other resolutions are EXPLICIT: the speaker names the chosen option.
5. Evidence is concrete and checkable (a measurement, a quote, a budget figure, a deadline). Keep the facts from the spec, but NEVER copy
   its wording. The spec describes what happens; it is not a script. Say it the way this person would: in their own words, in a different
   order, often across two or three short turns, sometimes interrupted or prompted by a question. A tagged line must not read like a
   report of the event, and most tagged lines should share few content words with the spec's sentence beyond names and numbers.
6. Continuity: refer back naturally to earlier meetings if any. People may occasionally misremember or contradict a small earlier detail
   (a date, a minor figure, who said something), and someone may or may not correct them. Never do this to the decision itself or to a
   fact in a listed event.
7. Do not invent other decisions about the topic. Unrelated agenda items are fine but must not touch the topic.
8. Never write the words "beat", "role", "reversal", "supersede", "lineage", or tag IDs inside speech.
9. Everyone in the cast speaks, in the voice of their listed style.

## Realism
- These are ordinary colleagues, not a sitcom. Some dryness is fine, but no running jokes, no catchphrases carried across turns or meetings,
  and no comic set pieces. Most turns are plain work talk.
- People are imperfect with numbers: rounding, approximating, checking a sheet, getting a sum slightly wrong or leaving it unfinished. Do not
  have anyone do neat exact arithmetic aloud as a habit.
- Use fictional company, vendor, product and venue names only. No real brands: no real companies, retailers, airlines, hotel chains,
  payment or card brands, email providers, software products or websites. Generic terms (a relational database, single sign-on, a
  spreadsheet, SOC 2 as a standard) and real city names are fine.
- Not every thread gets closed. People leave things vague, change subject, or lose the point.
- Use only the names under "Other people" in the spec for anyone outside the cast. Do not use these overused details: dentist, shoebox,
  biscuit, loft, Lisbon, parking ticket, fire warden, lift, coffee machine, lanyards, calendar migration.

## Variety
- Avoid stock constructions ("I think we should...", "Can I make the case for...", "I think this is a problem", "Let me decide: we're...").
  People propose, object, give reasons and reopen things in varied, often indirect ways. Keyword matching must not be enough to find an event.
- Pace it unevenly: unrelated items can dominate, with the topic squeezed in. Do not open with a neat agenda or close with a neat summary
  unless this person really would.
- Do not default to admin items, then the topic, then admin items. Start with the topic in some meetings, split it across the meeting in
  others, and vary how meetings open and end, including ending abruptly when someone has to leave.

## Before saving, check
Exact cast names; everyone speaks; every tag present, in order, on the named speaker; no tag IDs or banned words in speech; 60-120
utterances; tagged lines not copied from the spec; the decision state at the end matches "How this meeting goes".
